# Wingman onboarding in Spanish: rewrite for review (21 Nov 2026)

Serguei, thanks for the first read. You were right: the first Spanish version was translated line by line from English, so many sentences kept the English shape ("¿Muchas veces quieres…?").

This version is rewritten from what each screen is meant to do, not from the English sentence. **It is already in the app** (from the next build). Your corrections will go in on top of it. Screenshots of the current app are in `wingman-onboarding-es/`, numbered 01–24 like the sections below.

**What I need from you:** for each **New** line, say **OK** or write how a local would say it. A voice note is fine. Lines under "Kept" didn't change, so only flag them if something sounds off.

Target: neutral Latin American Spanish that also reads fine in Spain. Use "tú" everywhere and never "vosotros". If you're from Spain, a "we'd say it differently in Spain" note is still useful. Just say that's what it is.

## Two questions for you

1. **“Confianza” or “seguridad” for confidence?** The first version used *seguridad* everywhere. This rewrite uses *confianza (en ti mismo)* in onboarding, because bare *seguridad* can also mean safety. Which sounds right to you?
2. **“Acercarse” for approaching a woman.** The rest of the app uses *acercarte / acercamiento* (for example, “Registrar acercamiento” to log an approach). In onboarding I've mostly replaced it with *hablarle a una desconocida* and *dar el paso*. Does *acercarse* sound natural in this sense, or does it sound like a translation?

## Notes from a second read as a Spaniard

These are deliberate choices, not mistakes, but tell me if any of them grate:

- *videos* (Spain usually writes *vídeos*). Both are correct.
- *Mantén presionado* (Spain says *mantén pulsado*). iPhones in Latin America use *presionar*.
- *Configuración* (Spanish iPhones in Spain say *Ajustes*).
- *coqueteo* (in Spain, *ligar* is more common, but it is slangier).
- *chica* works everywhere. *mujer* is used where the tone is more serious.
